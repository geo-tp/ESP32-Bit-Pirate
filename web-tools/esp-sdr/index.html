Web ESP SDR help

  
What this tool does
Boot Bit Pirate in USB SDR adapter mode and connect to USB. Wideband Analyzer sweeps a frequency range to find activity; Live SDR gives a closer real-time view around one center frequency.

  
Step-by-step recipes
  1. Use the ESP SDR USB adapter
  2. Connect ESP SDR to the browser
  3. Scan the 2.4 GHz band with ESP SDR
  4. Inspect a 2.4 GHz channel with Live SDR
  5. Reduce noise and calibrate ESP SDR

  
ESP32-S3 RF limits
The ESP32-S3 radio is designed for 2.4 GHz, with 2400–2483.5 MHz as the most useful region. Low-level tuning can go beyond this for experimental tests (currently up to 100–6000 MHz), but sensitivity, PLL lock and level accuracy are not guaranteed outside 2.4 GHz. Treat out-of-band results as indications of activity, not calibrated measurements.

  
Antenna matters
The antenna strongly affects what you can see. A 2.4 GHz antenna may perform poorly outside its intended frequency range. Antenna type, matching, connectors and nearby objects can all change sensitivity, so a weak or missing signal may come from the antenna rather than the SDR processing. The antenna does not extend the tuning range; it determines how much RF energy reaches the radio.

  
Improving the view
Live SDR starts with maximum manual gain; Wideband starts with manual gain at 41 of 82, halfway along the slider. Adjust gain beside the frequency controls. Use Reduce noise to learn the current background with the antenna connected; signals present during learning may also be reduced. Advanced settings provides additional receiver and calibration controls when needed.
